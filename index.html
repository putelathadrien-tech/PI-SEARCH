How does it work?

Every letter is turned into numbers (digits stay as they are), then we look for that string of numbers in pi. Positions are counted from the first digit after the decimal point (3.1415… is position 1).

  • Alphabet: A=01, B=02, … Z=26. ADA becomes 010401.
  • Phone keypad: one digit per letter, like old phone keyboards. HELLO becomes 43556. Shorter codes mean longer words can be found.
  • Numbers: digits are searched as they are, so 2024 looks for 2024. Dates work too: 14/03/1999 looks for 14031999. You can mix letters and digits: R2D2 becomes 182042.

Big searches: the first 10 million digits load right away, so most answers are instant. If something isn't there, PI Search keeps reading pi 10 million digits at a time, up to 1,000,000,000, and stops as soon as it finds it.

Sentences: we first look for the whole sentence (spaces ignored). Long sentences need far more digits than exist here, so each word is also found on its own. If a word is still too long, it is split into the longest pieces that do appear in pi.

Digits of pi computed with the Chudnovsky algorithm. All searching happens in your browser.
